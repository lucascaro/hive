02Session colour — keeping it, spending less on it your rework

You're right that the colour has to stay, and it's load-bearing in a way the round-2 card didn't say: theme/layout.css:72,126 paints the grid tile's border with --session-color, and the focused tile gets a glow in it. The swatch's job is matching a sidebar row to a tile on screen. So the question isn't whether to show colour, it's whether a filled 10px chip in its own column is the cheapest way to show it. Four treatments — toggle between them on the composite above.

I changed my recommendation after seeing it assembled. Round 3 first proposed colouring the key hint, which is free in width. But A1 puts a coloured agent chip in the meta column, so outlining [1] in the session colour lands a second coloured mark right beside it — two unrelated colour systems, 6px apart, on every row. The right-edge bar keeps the session colour out of that column entirely, and G3c has just freed the right edge by moving “shared worktree” into the group panel. Flip between hint and edge on the composite and the noise difference is immediate.
This also retires the colorExplicit problem. Round 2's version needed a new daemon-side bit to tell auto-picked colours from chosen ones. None of these four need it: they show every session's colour, always. Pure CSS, no wire change.

03The patterns.md amendment you said amend

You chose to amend the rule rather than move the pulse to an edge bar. Here's the exact wording I'd commit alongside the change, so the next person to read the principle finds it consistent with the code. Approve or edit.

docs/design-docs/ui/patterns.md › Selection vs attention

Selection owns the row background and the left-edge bar. Attention never uses either channel — it speaks through the state icon and colour only.

Selection owns the row's static background and the left-edge bar. Attention never uses the left edge, and never paints a static background — it speaks through the state icon, its colour, and a slow background pulse. The two are distinguishable because one is still and the other moves: a selected row that also wants attention shows the selected ground with the pulse riding over it. Under prefers-reduced-motion the pulse degrades to a static tint at a lower alpha than --sel, so selection still reads as the stronger ground.

  • The last sentence is the part that needs care: with motion off, both channels become static backgrounds, so their alphas have to stay ordered. 9% attention against --sel clears that today; it's worth an assertion in the theme test rather than a comment.

Open question inside the question
What should clicking it open? Today it is an uncontrolled <input type="color"> that opens the OS picker — deliberately uncontrolled so the swatch does not snap back while you drag inside that picker (SessionRow.tsx:118). Keeping that is the zero-risk option: the button just proxies a click to a visually hidden input. The alternative is a small palette popover of the same colours pickColor() draws from, which is faster for the common case and loses arbitrary colours. I would keep the native input — it is the smaller diff and the behaviour you already have.
